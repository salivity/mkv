Can MKV Contain Apple ProRes RAW Video?

While the Matroska (MKV) container format is technically capable of encapsulating arbitrary video streams—including high-resolution Apple ProRes RAW data—it is not officially supported or practical for real-world workflows. ProRes RAW relies heavily on proprietary metadata structures embedded inside the QuickTime (.mov) container for debayering and color processing. Consequently, placing ProRes RAW video inside an MKV container results in unplayable files and broken workflows across virtually all professional video editing suites.

Technical Feasibility vs. Real-World Usability

At a fundamental level, the Matroska container specification is codec-agnostic and highly extensible. Tools like FFmpeg or MKVToolNix can theoretically wrap the underlying raw video bitstream and FourCC identifiers (such as aprn for ProRes RAW or aprh for ProRes RAW HQ) into an .mkv file.

However, technical encapsulation does not equal functional playback or editing support:

  • Metadata Dependency: Unlike standard compressed video, ProRes RAW is unprocessed sensor data. It requires specific metadata—such as Color Filter Array (CFA) Bayer patterns, camera color matrices, white balance values, and ISO settings—to be decoded (debayered) into viewable RGB images. This metadata is strictly mapped to QuickTime atom structures.
  • Lack of NLE Support: Major Non-Linear Editors (NLEs) such as Apple Final Cut Pro, Adobe Premiere Pro, and Avid Media Composer rely on Apple's official ProRes RAW decoder framework. This framework explicitly expects a .mov container and will not recognize or debayer ProRes RAW streams packed inside an MKV file.
  • Player Incompatibility: Standard media players that support MKV (such as VLC or MPV) lack the specialized debayering pipelines required to interpret raw sensor data from an MKV wrapper, resulting in playback errors or pure black frames.

Standard ProRes vs. ProRes RAW in MKV

It is important to distinguish standard Apple ProRes from ProRes RAW:

  • Standard ProRes (422, 4444, HQ, Proxy): These codecs contain pre-debayered, standard YUV or RGB video frames. MKV officially supports standard ProRes (using codec IDs such as V_PRORES), and these files can be muxed, played, and edited reliably in supporting environments.
  • ProRes RAW: Because it is not a traditional raster video format, it does not share the same container flexibility as standard ProRes.

Conclusion

To preserve full image quality, debayering capabilities, and compatibility with editing software, high-resolution Apple ProRes RAW footage must remain inside its native QuickTime (.mov) container. Wrapping ProRes RAW into an MKV file strips or isolates the critical metadata necessary to render the image properly.
