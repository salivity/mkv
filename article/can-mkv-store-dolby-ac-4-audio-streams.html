Can MKV Store Dolby AC-4 Audio Streams?

The Matroska (MKV) multimedia container fully supports storing Dolby AC-4 audio streams, the next-generation audio format primarily used in modern broadcast standards such as ATSC 3.0 and DVB. Because Matroska is an open and extensible container designed to encapsulate virtually any codec, official specifications include native mapping for AC-4. This article explores how AC-4 is stored within MKV files, the software tools used for muxing, and current playback considerations.

Matroska Specification for AC-4

The Matroska specification defines a specific Codec ID for Dolby AC-4: A_AC4. This identifier allows muxers, demuxers, and media players to recognize the stream as AC-4 data rather than generic private audio data. Because AC-4 carries complex metadata—including presentation configurations, dialogue enhancement parameters, and object-based audio positions—the MKV container encapsulates the raw bitstream without modifying its internal structure, preserving all broadcast features.

AC-4 in Next-Generation Broadcasting

Dolby AC-4 was designed to succeed AC-3 (Dolby Digital) and E-AC-3 (Dolby Digital Plus). It is a core component of ATSC 3.0 (NextGen TV) in North America and parts of Asia, as well as selected DVB-NGA deployments in Europe. AC-4 provides higher compression efficiency and advanced capabilities, such as:

  • Object-Based Immersive Audio: Channel beds combined with dynamic audio objects (e.g., overhead sounds).
  • Multi-Stream Audio Presentations: Multiple language tracks and commentary embedded efficiently in a single stream.
  • Advanced Dialogue Enhancement: Real-time user control over speech clarity relative to background sounds.

Storing captured broadcast streams in MKV allows users to preserve these features within an open, single-file format.

Muxing Support and Software Tools

Mainstream multimedia utilities support muxing AC-4 streams into Matroska files:

  • MKVToolNix (mkvmerge): Modern versions of MKVToolNix support the identification and muxing of raw AC-4 elementary streams into the .mkv format, assigning the appropriate A_AC4 Codec ID.
  • FFmpeg: FFmpeg includes support for parsing AC-4 streams and remuxing them from broadcast capture formats (such as MPEG-TS) directly into Matroska containers using stream copy (-c:a copy).

Container Support vs. Decoding Support

While the MKV container can store AC-4 without issue, playback depends entirely on the player software and hardware decoder. Storing the stream in MKV does not bypass Dolby licensing requirements:

  • Hardware Compatibility: Devices that support ATSC 3.0 or have native Dolby AC-4 certification (such as modern Smart TVs and select media boxes) can decode the audio directly from an MKV file or pass it through via eARC to a compatible receiver.
  • Software Decoders: Open-source decoders have limited or experimental support for AC-4 due to proprietary licensing. Software players that lack a licensed AC-4 decoder may fail to play the audio track or require external transcoding to formats like E-AC-3, AAC, or PCM.
