Can MKV Store 24-bit 96kHz High-Definition Audio?

The Matroska (MKV) container format fully supports high-definition audio tracks, including 24-bit/96kHz streams, without any downsampling or quality degradation. Because MKV acts strictly as an envelope for multimedia data rather than a compression algorithm, it preserves the exact bit depth, sample rate, and channel layout of the original audio track.

MKV as a Universal Container

MKV is a flexible, open-standard container format designed to hold an unlimited number of video, audio, picture, and subtitle tracks. It does not encode or compress data itself; instead, it packages already-encoded streams into a single file. Consequently, when you mux a 24-bit/96kHz audio stream into an MKV container, the file stores the raw stream bit-for-bit without applying conversion, compression, or downsampling.

Supported High-Definition Audio Formats

MKV supports virtually all modern lossless and high-resolution audio codecs capable of carrying 24-bit/96kHz (and even 24-bit/192kHz or 32-bit float) audio:

  • FLAC (Free Lossless Audio Codec): Natively supports high-resolution audio up to 32-bit/655kHz.
  • Linear PCM (LPCM): Uncompressed, studio-quality audio streams.
  • Dolby TrueHD: A lossless multichannel codec widely used on Blu-ray media, capable of 24-bit/96kHz across multiple channels.
  • DTS-HD Master Audio (DTS-HD MA): A bit-for-bit lossless format supporting high-resolution surround sound.
  • ALAC (Apple Lossless Audio Codec): Lossless format supporting up to 24-bit/192kHz.

Requirements for Lossless Playback

While the MKV file will preserve the 24-bit/96kHz stream intact, actual playback at native resolution depends on your playback hardware and software pipeline:

  1. Software Players: Media players like VLC, MPC-HC, mpv, or Kodi must be configured to pass the audio stream without software-level resampling.
  2. Audio Drivers and Operating Systems: Windows (via WASAPI Exclusive Mode or ASIO) or macOS/Linux audio settings must allow the exact sample rate and bit depth to bypass the OS audio mixer.
  3. DAC and Receiver: The external Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC) or AV receiver must natively support 24-bit/96kHz decoding.
