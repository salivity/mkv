Can MKV Store DTS-HD High Resolution Audio?

Yes, the Matroska (MKV) container format fully supports audio streams encoded in the DTS-HD High Resolution (DTS-HD HR) format. Due to its open-standard and extensible architecture, MKV can encapsulate virtually any modern audio codec, including both lossy and lossless high-resolution multichannel audio streams. This article explains how MKV handles DTS-HD HR streams, the technical mechanics of the format, and the requirements for proper playback.

How MKV Handles DTS-HD HR Audio

The Matroska container structure does not alter or re-encode audio data; it simply acts as a wrapper. DTS-HD High Resolution Audio is built with a standard DTS "core" track along with an extension substream that provides higher bitrates (up to 6 Mbps on Blu-ray) and up to 7.1 discrete channels at 96 kHz/24-bit resolution.

When multiplexed into an MKV file using tools like MKVToolNix, the entire DTS-HD HR stream—both the core and the extension—is preserved intact within the audio track.

Backward Compatibility and Playback

Because DTS-HD HR uses a core-plus-extension architecture, MKV files containing this audio format offer high compatibility:

  • Bitstreaming (Passthrough): When connected to an AV receiver via HDMI, compatible media players can bitstream the complete DTS-HD HR track untouched, allowing the receiver to decode the high-resolution audio.
  • Software Decoding: Media players such as VLC, MPC-HC, and Kodi equipped with FFmpeg-based audio decoders can decode the full DTS-HD HR stream directly to multichannel PCM.
  • Legacy Devices: If an audio device or optical connection (S/PDIF) cannot process the high-resolution extension, the player or receiver automatically ignores the extension and decodes the standard lossy DTS 5.1 core.

Muxing DTS-HD HR into MKV

Adding a DTS-HD HR stream to an MKV file requires no proprietary tools. Standard Matroska software, such as mkvmerge (part of MKVToolNix), recognizes DTS-HD HR bitstreams extracted from Blu-ray discs or standalone audio files (such as .dtshd or .dts). The software properly flags the audio track header, ensuring media splitters and decoders recognize the format accurately upon playback.
