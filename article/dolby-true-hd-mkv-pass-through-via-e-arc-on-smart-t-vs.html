Dolby TrueHD MKV Pass-Through via eARC on Smart TVs

Modern smart TVs equipped with enhanced Audio Return Channel (eARC) have the physical bandwidth to transmit lossless audio formats like Dolby TrueHD. However, when playing local MKV files directly through the television's internal media player or built-in apps (such as Plex or Emby), TrueHD pass-through is almost universally blocked. Understanding why this limitation exists and how smart TVs process these audio streams is essential for setting up a proper home theater environment.

The Internal App Limitation

While eARC hardware supports high-bandwidth formats up to 37 Mbps—easily accommodating the bitrates required for Dolby TrueHD and Dolby Atmos—the restriction lies within the smart TV operating systems (such as LG webOS, Samsung Tizen, or Google TV on Sony and TCL).

Television manufacturers design internal audio pipelines primarily for streaming services, which use lossy formats like Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC-3). As a result, the internal media players and operating system decoders lack the licensing or system-level capability to route raw, lossless TrueHD bitstreams from an internal application (USB playback or local network streaming) out through the eARC port.

How Smart TVs Handle TrueHD Playback

When attempting to play an MKV container containing a Dolby TrueHD track via an internal TV app, the TV will typically handle the audio in one of four ways:

  • Fallback to Dolby Digital/AC-3: If the MKV file contains an embedded secondary lossy Dolby Digital track (or if the media server extracts it), the TV will play that lossy track instead.
  • Server-Side Transcoding: If using a media server like Plex or Jellyfin, the server detects that the TV cannot decode TrueHD and automatically transcodes the audio to multi-channel PCM, Dolby Digital, or AAC.
  • Downmixing to Stereo PCM: Some TVs decode the TrueHD stream internally and downmix it to a two-channel stereo PCM signal before sending it over eARC.
  • No Audio: In the worst-case scenario where the TV cannot decode or route the track and no fallback exists, playback results in complete silence.

Internal Playback vs. External HDMI Pass-Through

There is a major distinction between playing an MKV file from an internal app versus an external source device:

  • Internal Apps (Fails): Playing an MKV file from a USB drive plugged into the TV or via an internal app will not pass TrueHD over eARC.
  • External Devices (Succeeds): Connecting an external media player (such as an Nvidia Shield TV Pro, a dedicated Blu-ray player, or an HTPC) directly to an HDMI input on the TV allows the TV to act purely as a pass-through bridge. The TV accepts the raw TrueHD bitstream from the external device and sends it untouched via the eARC port to an AV receiver or soundbar.

The Solution for TrueHD MKV Playback

To achieve full, uncompressed Dolby TrueHD (including TrueHD with Dolby Atmos) playback from MKV files, bypass the smart TV's native media player entirely. The standard solution is to connect an external streaming device that natively supports TrueHD bitstreaming directly to an eARC-capable AV receiver/soundbar, or to the TV's HDMI input set to "Bitstream / Pass-Through" mode.
