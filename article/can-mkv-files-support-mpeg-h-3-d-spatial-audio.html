Can MKV Files Support MPEG-H 3D Spatial Audio?

The Matroska (MKV) container format fully supports the encapsulation of spatial audio encoded in MPEG-H 3D Audio. As an advanced multimedia container, Matroska is designed to carry Next-Generation Audio (NGA) streams, including object-based and channel-based immersive audio formats. This article details how the MKV specification integrates MPEG-H 3D Audio, its technical implementation via MHAS packets, and current software and hardware compatibility.

MKV and MPEG-H 3D Audio Integration

The Matroska specification accommodates MPEG-H 3D Audio (ISO/IEC 23008-3) through dedicated Codec IDs. MPEG-H 3D Audio is transported inside the MKV container encapsulated in an MPEG-H 3D Audio Stream (MHAS) format.

To identify these streams correctly, Matroska utilizes specific Codec IDs:

  • A_MPEGH/ISO/SP: Matroska identifier for the MPEG-H 3D Audio Simple Profile.
  • A_MPEGH/ISO/LC: Matroska identifier for the Low Complexity Profile.
  • A_MPEGH/ISO/BL: Matroska identifier for the Baseline Profile.

The container stores the raw MHAS packets as audio frames, preserving all spatial metadata, audio objects, channel beds, and Higher-Order Ambisonics (HOA) signals without loss of spatial positioning.

Key Features Supported Inside MKV

Encapsulating MPEG-H 3D Audio in MKV retains the full suite of interactive and immersive capabilities provided by the codec:

  • Object-Based Audio: Dynamic audio objects and their real-time 3D coordinate metadata are maintained.
  • Channel and Scene-Based Audio: Support for standard 5.1/7.1 beds up to 22.2 configurations and Ambisonics.
  • User Interactivity: Metadata allowing end users to adjust dialogue levels, switch languages, or modify audio element positioning remains functional during playback.
  • Binaural Rendering: Headphone-rendered 3D spatial audio profiles defined within the MPEG-H payload can be processed directly by compatible renderers.

Tools and Playback Compatibility

Muxing and demuxing MPEG-H 3D Audio in MKV is supported across several modern multimedia frameworks:

  • FFmpeg: Recent versions of FFmpeg and libavformat support the demuxing and remuxing of MPEG-H audio streams within the Matroska container.
  • MKVToolNix: Current builds of mkvmerge recognize MPEG-H elementary streams and can package them into an .mkv file alongside high-definition video formats such as HEVC/H.265 or AV1.
  • Media Players and Decoders: Media players built on FFmpeg (such as VLC or mpv) can demux the stream, passing the raw bitstream to an MPEG-H-capable AVR/soundbar or using an MPEG-H software decoder library to render spatial audio locally.
