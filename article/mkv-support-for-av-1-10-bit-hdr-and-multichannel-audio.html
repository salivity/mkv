MKV Support for AV1 10-Bit HDR and Multichannel Audio

The Matroska (MKV) multimedia container fully supports AV1 video encoded with 10-bit High Dynamic Range (HDR) alongside multichannel surround sound audio. Because MKV is an open and extensible format, it is engineered to multiplex virtually any combination of modern video codecs, high-bit-depth color metadata, and multi-stream audio tracks into a single file without compromising data integrity or playback synchronization.

AV1 and 10-Bit HDR Compatibility in MKV

The Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia) developed the AV1 codec with native support for high-efficiency compression, 10-bit and 12-bit color depths, and advanced HDR formats. The Matroska specification integrates AV1 via the standard codec identifier V_AV1.

When storing 10-bit HDR content, MKV holds both the core AV1 bitstream and the necessary dynamic or static color metadata:

  • Color Primaries and Transfer Characteristics: MKV contains specific header elements for BT.2020 color spaces and transfer functions such as SMPTE ST 2084 (PQ) for HDR10/Dolby Vision or ARIB STD-B67 for Hybrid Log-Gamma (HLG).
  • Mastering Display Metadata: Static HDR parameters, including Mastering Display Color Primaries, white point coordinates, and maximum/minimum luminance values, are embedded directly into the container's Colour element or preserved within the AV1 sequence headers.

Multichannel Audio Support

MKV is one of the most capable containers for multichannel and object-based audio. It can store surround sound configurations ranging from standard 5.1 and 7.1 setups up to complex spatial audio formats without requiring downmixing.

Compatible multichannel audio codecs within an MKV file include:

  • Lossless Formats: Dolby TrueHD (with or without Dolby Atmos metadata), DTS-HD Master Audio, DTS:X, FLAC, and uncompressed multichannel LPCM.
  • Lossy Formats: Dolby Digital (AC-3), Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC-3, including Atmos extensions), DTS, multichannel AAC, and Opus (supporting native 5.1 and 7.1 mapping).

Multiplexing and Playback Requirements

Creating an MKV file with 10-bit HDR AV1 and multichannel audio is supported across all modern multiplexing software, including MKVToolNix, FFmpeg, and HandBrake.

For seamless playback, the end-user environment requires:

  1. Software/Hardware Decoding: A player (such as mpv, VLC, or Kodi) paired with a GPU or CPU capable of decoding 10-bit AV1 video.
  2. Display and Passthrough: An HDR-capable display (HDR10/Dolby Vision compliant) and an AV receiver or soundbar capable of decoding the chosen multichannel audio stream via HDMI passthrough (eARC) or PCM decoding.
