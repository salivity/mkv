Can MKV Support Dolby TrueHD with Dolby Atmos?

The Matroska (MKV) container fully supports the encapsulation of Dolby TrueHD audio combined with Dolby Atmos spatial metadata. Because Dolby Atmos is not a standalone audio format but rather an extension layer embedded directly into the lossless Dolby TrueHD bitstream, MKV packages the entire audio structure into a single, cohesive audio track without stripping the spatial metadata.

How MKV Handles TrueHD with Dolby Atmos

Dolby TrueHD uses Meridian Lossless Packing (MLP) technology to deliver uncompressed multi-channel sound (typically 5.1 or 7.1). When an Atmos mix is created for consumer media, the spatial metadata—which defines dynamic 3D audio objects and their positioning coordinates—is multiplexed directly inside the core TrueHD stream.

The Matroska container utilizes the standard A_TRUEHD codec identifier for these streams. When tools like MakeMKV or MKVToolNix multiplex a Blu-ray or UHD Blu-ray disc into an MKV file, the Dolby TrueHD track is copied byte-for-byte. The MKV specification does not alter, re-encode, or separate the Atmos metadata from the base TrueHD channel data.

Playback Requirements for TrueHD Atmos from MKV

While the MKV container successfully stores the combined TrueHD and Atmos data, reproducing the object-based spatial audio requires compatible hardware and software configurations:

  • Audio Passthrough (Bitstreaming): The media player software (such as Kodi, Plex, VLC, or MPC-BE) must be configured to bitstream the raw TrueHD audio over HDMI to an external audio processor rather than decoding it internally.
  • HDMI Connectivity: Because Dolby TrueHD is high-bandwidth and lossless, it cannot be transmitted over legacy optical (TOSLink) or standard ARC connections. An HDMI connection directly to an AV receiver (AVR) or soundbar, or an HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel) setup from the TV, is required.
  • AV Receiver / Soundbar Decoding: The connected AVR or soundbar must feature a certified Dolby Atmos decoder to unpack the spatial metadata and map the audio objects to your specific speaker layout.

Backward Compatibility

If a playback device or AV receiver does not support Dolby Atmos decoding, the TrueHD stream inside the MKV remains fully functional. The hardware will simply ignore the spatial object metadata and decode the traditional 7.1 or 5.1 lossless Dolby TrueHD channel bed. Additionally, many TrueHD tracks embedded within MKV files retain an embedded standard Dolby Digital (AC-3) core track, ensuring playback on legacy equipment.
