Can MKV Encapsulate DTS Express Audio Tracks?

This article explores whether the Matroska (MKV) multimedia container supports the encapsulation of DTS Express (DTS-HD LBR) audio tracks, typically used for secondary audio on Blu-ray media. It details the technical compatibility of the container format, how multiplexing tools handle these streams, and key considerations for downstream playback and decoding.

Yes, an MKV container can encapsulate DTS Express audio tracks. Matroska is an open and extensible container format designed to hold virtually any modern audio, video, or subtitle codec, including proprietary and secondary audio streams derived from optical media specifications.

Understanding DTS Express

DTS Express, formally known as DTS-HD Low Bit Rate (LBR), is an audio codec engineered by DTS to deliver multi-channel audio at highly efficient bitrates (typically between 48 kbps and 768 kbps). In the Blu-ray specification, it is the primary standard for secondary audio tracks, such as director commentaries, interactive audio, picture-in-picture tracks, and supplementary language options.

How MKV Handles DTS Express

When multiplexing DTS Express into an MKV file, the Matroska container utilizes the standard A_DTS Codec ID. Because the container acts strictly as an envelope for the bitstream, it does not alter or re-encode the audio packets.

Standard multiplexing software—most notably MKVToolNix (mkvmerge), MakeMKV, and FFmpeg—fully recognize DTS-HD LBR streams:

  • MakeMKV directly preserves secondary DTS Express tracks when backing up Blu-ray discs into MKV files.
  • MKVToolNix accepts raw DTS-HD LBR elementary streams or extracts them from transport streams (M2TS) and packages them into Matroska tracks seamlessly.
  • FFmpeg can read, remux, and package DTS-HD LBR tracks directly into MKV without requiring re-encoding.

Playback and Decoding Considerations

While the MKV container provides complete support for storing the format, successful playback depends on the media player and decoder:

  1. Software Players: Modern software players using FFmpeg or libavcodec (such as VLC Media Player, MPC-HC, MPC-BE, and Kodi) feature built-in decoders for DTS-HD LBR, allowing these tracks to decode and downmix to PCM without issue.
  2. Hardware Players and Passthrough: Standalone media streamers, smart TVs, or older AV receivers (AVRs) may not natively support DTS Express decoding or bitstream passthrough over HDMI or optical connections. In these setups, the media player must decode the stream internally to PCM rather than sending the raw bitstream to the AVR.
