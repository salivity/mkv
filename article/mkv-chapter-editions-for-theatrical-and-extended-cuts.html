MKV Chapter Editions for Theatrical and Extended Cuts

The Matroska (MKV) container fully supports storing multiple chapter editions, making it possible to include both theatrical and extended cuts of a movie within a single file. By utilizing Matroska's native "Edition" metadata and "Ordered Chapters" features, users can define distinct playback timelines that rearrange, add, or skip specific scenes without needing to encode duplicate video streams.

How Matroska Editions Work

In the Matroska specification, a chapter structure can contain multiple EditionEntry elements. Each edition represents a different version or cut of the media, such as:

  • Theatrical Cut
  • Director's Cut
  • Extended Cut
  • Unrated Version

Each edition is assigned a unique identifier (EditionUID) and can have attributes such as EditionFlagDefault (to set which version plays automatically) and EditionFlagHidden.

Ordered Chapters and Seamless Branching

MKV achieves multiple cuts efficiently through Ordered Chapters:

  1. Standard Playlists: If the full extended footage is already in the file's primary video track, the theatrical edition simply defines chapters that skip over the extended or alternate scenes. The extended edition defines chapters that play the entire timeline sequentially.
  2. Segment Linking (Seamless Branching): MKV can reference external MKV files (segments) or different timestamps within the same file. Unique scenes for the extended cut are only stored once, and the player branches to those timestamps when the extended edition is selected.

This approach saves significant disk space compared to keeping two separate, full-length video files.

Software for Creating and Playing MKV Editions

To create and manage multiple chapter editions in an MKV file:

  • Creation: MKVToolNix (specifically the Chapter Editor tool) is the standard software used to create, edit, and flag different EditionEntry elements and ordered chapters.
  • Playback: Media players that fully support the Matroska specification for ordered chapters and editions include mpv, MPC-HC (using LAV Filters), and VLC Media Player. In these players, users can right-click the video and navigate to the "Chapters" or "Titles/Editions" menu to toggle between the theatrical and extended versions instantly.
