Difference Between MKV and MKS Files

The Matroska multimedia container format uses distinct file extensions to designate the specific types of media streams contained within a file. While both MKV and MKS are based on the same underlying open-standard framework, they serve fundamentally different functions: MKV (Matroska Video) is a complete multimedia container designed to hold video, audio, and subtitle streams together, whereas MKS (Matroska Subtitles) is a specialized container used exclusively for storing standalone subtitle tracks.

The Matroska Framework

Matroska is an open-standard, extensible container format based on EBML (Extensible Binary Meta Language). Rather than being a codec itself, Matroska acts as an envelope that can hold an unlimited number of video, audio, picture, or subtitle tracks within a single file. To help operating systems and media players quickly identify the primary content of a file, the Matroska project established four primary extensions:

  • .MKV: Video (which may also contain audio and subtitles)
  • .MKA: Audio-only
  • .MKS: Subtitles-only
  • .MK3D: Stereoscopic (3D) video

What Is an MKV File?

The .mkv extension is the most widely recognized Matroska format. It is intended for full audiovisual media, commonly used for movies, television shows, and video clips.

An MKV file typically includes:

  • At least one video track (using codecs such as H.264, HEVC, AV1, or VP9).
  • One or more audio tracks (such as AAC, AC3, DTS, or FLAC), often in multiple languages.
  • Multiple subtitle tracks (such as SRT, ASS/SSA, or PGS).
  • Metadata including chapter markers, menu systems, and cover art.

Because of its broad adoption, MKV is supported natively by most modern media players, smart TVs, and streaming platforms.

What Is an MKS File?

The .mks extension is a dedicated subtitle container. It contains no video or audio streams. Instead, it utilizes the Matroska structure to package raw subtitle data alongside Matroska's native timing, metadata, and attachment features.

An MKS file typically includes:

  • Subtitle streams in formats like SubRip (SRT), Advanced SubStation Alpha (ASS/SSA), Universal Subtitle Format (USF), or bitmap-based subtitles like VobSub.
  • Embedded font files used to render styled text properly across different devices.
  • Precise timecodes and chapter synchronization markers.

MKS files are primarily used by video editors, subtitling teams, and archivists who want to distribute or store complex, styled external subtitle sets without relying on plain text formats that cannot package custom fonts or multiple language tracks into a single file.

Key Differences Summary

  • Primary Content: MKV files carry video data alongside optional audio and subtitle tracks. MKS files carry only subtitle data.
  • Playback Compatibility: MKV files are universally recognized by standard media players. MKS files are niche and usually require advanced media players (like VLC or MPC-HC) or dedicated subtitling software (like Aegisub) to open and render.
  • Use Case: MKV is used for general video playback and distribution. MKS is used as an external, multi-track subtitle resource designed to be loaded alongside a separate video file.
