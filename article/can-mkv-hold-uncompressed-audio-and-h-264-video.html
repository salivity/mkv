Can MKV Hold Uncompressed Audio and H.264 Video?

The Matroska (MKV) container format fully supports encapsulating uncompressed audio tracks alongside high-bitrate H.264 video streams. Because MKV is an open-standard, highly extensible multimedia container rather than a codec, it does not impose restrictive limits on data rates or audio-video combinations. This article outlines how MKV handles uncompressed audio, its capability with high-bitrate H.264 video, and the practical playback considerations associated with this configuration.

Matroska Container Architecture

MKV is built upon the Extensible Binary Meta Language (EBML), allowing it to wrap virtually any combination of video, audio, and subtitle streams. It acts as an envelope that synchronizes and multiplexes independent streams without altering the underlying data.

High-Bitrate H.264 Video Support

H.264 (also known as AVC or MPEG-4 Part 10) is natively supported within the MKV specification. The container imposes no artificial ceiling on video bitrates, frame rates, or resolutions. Whether the stream is a standard 1080p encode at 10 Mbps or a visually lossless High 4:4:4 Predictive profile running at several hundred megabits per second, MKV accurately stores the video frames, timing metadata, and keyframe indexes required for smooth playback and seeking.

Encapsulating Uncompressed Audio

MKV has robust native support for raw and uncompressed audio formats:

  • Linear PCM (LPCM): Uncompressed pulse-code modulation data—including 16-bit, 24-bit, and 32-bit depths at sample rates ranging from 44.1 kHz to 192 kHz or higher—can be muxed directly into an MKV track.
  • Multi-Channel Configurations: Uncompressed audio in MKV is not limited to stereo; it easily accommodates multi-channel layouts such as 5.1 and 7.1 surround sound.
  • WAV / AIFF Direct Ingestion: Multiplexing tools like mkvmerge can ingest standard .wav files and embed the raw audio frames directly into the MKV file without any lossy re-encoding.

Lossless Alternatives

While raw PCM is fully supported, uncompressed audio takes up substantial storage space. For this reason, users frequently pair high-bitrate H.264 video with lossless compression codecs inside MKV, such as:

  • FLAC (Free Lossless Audio Codec): Provides bit-perfect reconstruction of PCM audio at roughly half the file size.
  • Dolby TrueHD / DTS-HD Master Audio: Lossless surround formats commonly extracted from Blu-ray discs that preserve master-quality uncompressed sound with optimized container overhead.

Playback and Hardware Compatibility

Most modern software media players (such as VLC, MPV, and MPC-HC) and media servers (like Plex and Jellyfin) can parse MKV files containing uncompressed PCM and H.264 video without issue. However, older standalone hardware players, smart TVs, or legacy AV receivers might have firmware limitations regarding uncompressed LPCM within an MKV wrapper, even though the container itself supports it. In standard computing environments, the pairing functions seamlessly.
