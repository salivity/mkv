Can MKV Support 5.1.4 Immersive Audio Tracks?

The Matroska (MKV) multimedia container fully supports multichannel audio streams configured for 5.1.4 immersive audio setups. This article explains how the MKV format handles object-based and multichannel audio, the specific codecs used to achieve a 5.1.4 configuration, and the necessary playback chain required to render 3D height channels properly.

How MKV Containers Handle Immersive Audio

MKV is a container format designed to hold virtually any type and number of video, audio, and subtitle streams. It does not compress or modify audio data on its own; instead, it encapsulates standard surround sound tracks along with the spatial metadata required for immersive speaker configurations.

A 5.1.4 layout consists of five ear-level surround speakers, one subwoofer, and four overhead or height speakers. MKV supports this configuration primarily through object-based audio codecs rather than ten raw, discrete channels.

Supported Codecs for 5.1.4 Playback

To output sound to a 5.1.4 system, the MKV file must contain an audio stream encoded in an immersive audio format:

  • Dolby TrueHD with Dolby Atmos: A lossless audio codec commonly found on Blu-ray discs. The base 7.1 or 5.1 channel bed is combined with spatial audio objects that your audio receiver renders to the four height channels.
  • Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC-3) with Dolby Atmos: A lossy, higher-efficiency format commonly used by streaming services. It contains metadata that maps audio to height channels in a 5.1.4 setup.
  • DTS-HD Master Audio with DTS:X: A lossless alternative to Dolby Atmos. DTS:X uses an object-based system to dynamically place sounds into overhead channels.
  • Multichannel LPCM: While MKV can hold uncompressed, discrete 10-channel LPCM audio tracks, standard consumer AV receivers typically require Dolby Atmos or DTS:X bitstreams to accurately assign audio to overhead height speakers.

Playback Requirements for 5.1.4 Audio

Playing a 5.1.4 audio track from an MKV file requires a compatible hardware and software chain:

  1. Software Player: The media player (such as Kodi, Plex, MPC-HC with LAV Filters, or VLC) must be set to "audio passthrough" or "bitstreaming." This allows the raw audio stream to bypass software decoding and reach the receiver untouched.
  2. Connection: The playback device must connect to the sound system via HDMI (HDMI 2.0 or higher, or HDMI eARC) to support high-bandwidth formats like TrueHD and DTS-HD MA. Optical (TOSLink) cables do not possess enough bandwidth for immersive audio.
  3. AV Receiver or Processor: The audio processor must support Dolby Atmos or DTS:X decoding and be configured with nine listener-level/height channels and one subwoofer output.
