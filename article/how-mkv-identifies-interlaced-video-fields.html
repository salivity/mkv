How MKV Identifies Interlaced Video Fields

The Matroska (MKV) multimedia container identifies interlaced video content through specific metadata elements stored inside the video track headers. By defining standard EBML (Extensible Binary Meta Language) elements such as FlagInterlaced and FieldOrder, an MKV file instructs decoders, splitters, and media players on whether the video frames are progressive or interlaced, and in what sequence the odd and even scan lines should be displayed.

The Role of EBML Video Track Headers

In an MKV container, track configuration is defined within the TrackEntry master element. When a track is defined as video, it contains a nested Video element. Inside this block, Matroska provides dedicated flags that signal the scan type and field parity of the video stream without requiring the player to decode the underlying video bitstream first.

Key MKV Interlacing Flags

Matroska uses two primary EBML elements to signal interlacing parameters:

1. FlagInterlaced (0x9A)

The FlagInterlaced element is a basic flag that declares whether the video stream contains progressive frames or interlaced fields. It accepts the following integer values:

  • 0 (Undetermined): The container does not declare whether the content is progressive or interlaced. The decoder must rely on codec-level metadata (such as H.264 VUI or MPEG-2 sequence headers).
  • 1 (Interlaced): The video stream contains interlaced fields.
  • 2 (Progressive): The video stream consists entirely of progressive frames.

2. FieldOrder (0x9D)

To prevent visual artifacts and field-order inversion, modern Matroska specifications include the FieldOrder element. This element specifies the temporal and spatial order in which the fields were captured and should be rendered.

The standard values for FieldOrder are:

  • 0 (Progressive): Scanned sequentially from top to bottom as full frames.
  • 1 (Top Field First - TFF): The top field (even-numbered lines) represents the earlier point in time and should be displayed before the bottom field.
  • 2 (Undetermined): The video is interlaced, but the container does not specify the field priority.
  • 6 (Bottom Field First - BFF): The bottom field (odd-numbered lines) represents the earlier point in time and should be displayed before the top field.
  • 9 (Bottom Field First - Swapped): Indicates interlaced content where the bottom field is temporal first, but fields are packed in reverse order in the buffer.
  • 14 (Top Field First - Swapped): Indicates interlaced content where the top field is temporal first, but fields are packed in reverse order in the buffer.

Container Flags vs. Codec Bitstream Headers

Many video codecs (such as H.264/AVC, H.265/HEVC, and MPEG-2) store their own interlacing information within their elementary bitstreams. However, parsing these bitstream parameters requires the demuxer or decoder to initialize the codec engine and parse specific sequence parameter sets (SPS).

By declaring FlagInterlaced and FieldOrder directly in the MKV container headers, demuxers can instantly route the stream to appropriate hardware deinterlacing filters and video renderers before decoding begins. When container flags and bitstream flags conflict, most compliant video players prioritize the container-level flags to allow users to override incorrectly encoded bitstream headers without re-encoding the video track.
