Can MKV Store Daala Encoded HD Video?

The Matroska (MKV) multimedia container is technically capable of storing high-definition (HD) video encoded with the experimental, open-source Daala video codec. Thanks to Matroska's flexible and codec-agnostic architecture, users and developers can encapsulate emerging, experimental, or non-standard bitstreams alongside standard audio and subtitle tracks. While Daala was primarily developed as a research and development effort by the Xiph.Org Foundation and Mozilla before contributing its core technologies to the AV1 standard, it can be packaged inside MKV files for testing, archiving, and high-definition playback.

Matroska’s Extensible Architecture

Matroska was designed from the ground up to support virtually any audiovisual format. Unlike more rigid containers, MKV does not impose strict limitations on the underlying compression format, resolution, or frame rate. It uses an extensible binary format known as EBML (Extensible Binary Meta Language), which allows developers to define custom Codec IDs for experimental formats like Daala. Consequently, storing high-definition video streams—whether 720p, 1080p, or higher—is natively supported within the container framework.

The Daala Codec and High-Definition Video

Daala was engineered specifically to push the boundaries of high-efficiency video coding for high-definition streaming and media playback. It incorporated experimental techniques such as lapped transforms and advanced perceptual vector quantization to avoid traditional blocking artifacts. Because Daala was built with high-definition and ultra-high-definition media in mind, pairing its bitstream with the feature-rich MKV container provided developers with a robust environment for testing multi-track audio, metadata, and subtitle integration without altering the raw compressed video stream.

Practical Implementation and Tooling

While native, out-of-the-box support depends on the specific build of playback software and muxing utilities, tools utilizing development libraries from projects like FFmpeg or customized Matroska toolkits could mux and demux Daala streams into .mkv files. In standard consumer environments, Daala was more commonly encapsulated in the Ogg container (.ogv) for reference testing, but MKV remains fully capable of holding the bitstream provided the demuxer recognizes the corresponding codec mapping.

Today, while the Daala project has concluded and its innovations have transitioned into the Alliance for Open Media's AV1 codec, storing Daala-encoded HD video in an MKV file remains entirely feasible from a structural and technical standpoint.
