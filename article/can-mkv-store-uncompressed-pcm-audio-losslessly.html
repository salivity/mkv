Can MKV Store Uncompressed PCM Audio Losslessly?

The Matroska Multimedia Container (MKV) fully supports uncompressed Pulse-Code Modulation (PCM) audio streams with zero loss in audio quality. Because MKV is an open-standard container format rather than an audio encoder, it can encapsulate raw PCM data directly into its structure without altering, compressing, or re-encoding the original bitstream. This makes MKV an ideal choice for archiving and playing back bit-perfect, studio-grade audio alongside high-definition video.

Understanding the MKV Container and PCM Audio

To understand why no quality loss occurs, it is essential to distinguish between a container format and an audio codec:

  • MKV (Matroska): A flexible multimedia container designed to hold an unlimited number of video, audio, picture, and subtitle tracks within a single file.
  • PCM (Pulse-Code Modulation): The standard method used to digitally represent analog signals in an uncompressed, raw format. It is the underlying standard for standard Audio CDs (16-bit/44.1 kHz) as well as high-resolution audio (24-bit/96 kHz or 192 kHz).

When you add a PCM stream to an MKV file, the process is called multiplexing (muxing). Muxing does not alter the audio waveform or discard data; it simply places the raw PCM samples into Matroska data blocks alongside any associated video or subtitle tracks.

How MKV Handles PCM Streams

The Matroska specification includes dedicated codec identifiers for various PCM configurations. When raw audio is encapsulated in MKV, it is mapped using standardized Codec IDs, including:

  • A_PCM/INT/LIT: Uncompressed integer PCM (Little-Endian)
  • A_PCM/INT/BIG: Uncompressed integer PCM (Big-Endian)
  • A_PCM/FLOAT/IEEE: IEEE floating-point PCM

Because these mappings strictly store the digital samples as they were originally recorded, demuxing the audio stream from the MKV container at a later stage yields an identical, bit-for-bit duplicate of the source PCM file.

Key Advantages of Muxing PCM into MKV

  1. Bit-Perfect Preservation: Bit depth (e.g., 16-bit, 24-bit, 32-bit), sample rate (up to 192 kHz and beyond), and channel layouts (mono, stereo, 5.1, 7.1) remain completely intact.
  2. Multi-Track Synchronization: MKV handles precise timestamping, ensuring multi-channel uncompressed audio stays in frame-accurate synchronization with the video track.
  3. Broad Compatibility: Major media players such as VLC, MPC-HC, MPV, and FFmpeg-based tools natively read and decode PCM audio encapsulated inside MKV files.

PCM vs. Lossless Compression in MKV

While raw PCM within an MKV container guarantees pristine quality, it results in large file sizes. Users seeking identical audio quality with reduced storage requirements often utilize lossless compression codecs such as FLAC or ALAC within the MKV container. Lossless codecs compress audio data mathematically—similar to a ZIP file—reducing file size by 30% to 60% while maintaining bit-perfect parity with the original PCM stream upon playback.

However, if direct hardware or software workflow requirements mandate uncompressed PCM, MKV encapsulates the stream reliably with no quality degradation.
