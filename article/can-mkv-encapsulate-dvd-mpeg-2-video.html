Can MKV Encapsulate DVD MPEG-2 Video?

The Matroska (MKV) container format fully supports encapsulating MPEG-2 video streams extracted directly from DVD-Video structures without requiring re-encoding. This process, known as remuxing, extracts the original elementary video, audio, and subtitle streams from DVD VOB files and packages them into a single MKV file. By doing so, users preserve the exact visual quality, audio fidelity, and structure of the original disc in a modern, widely compatible format.

How Matroska Handles DVD MPEG-2 Streams

Matroska is designed to be an open, highly extensible, and codec-agnostic multimedia container. Unlike more restrictive containers, MKV natively supports ISO/IEC 13818-2 (MPEG-2 Video) elementary streams.

When converting a DVD to MKV:

  • No Quality Loss: The raw MPEG-2 video bitstream is transferred bit-for-bit, meaning there is zero generation loss or compression artifacting.
  • Aspect Ratio Preservation: DVDs commonly use non-square pixels (anamorphic 720x480 for NTSC or 720x576 for PAL) to display 4:3 or 16:9 content. The MKV container correctly preserves and handles display aspect ratio (DAR) and pixel aspect ratio (PAR) metadata flags, ensuring proper playback dimensions on modern displays.
  • Interlacing Metadata: MKV stores the original interlaced fields and scan type flags as they exist on the DVD, allowing hardware and software decoders to apply appropriate deinterlacing filters during playback.

Preserving Associated DVD Assets

Beyond the MPEG-2 video stream, an MKV container can package all accompanying components found within the DVD-Video structure:

  • Audio Tracks: Standard DVD audio formats—including Dolby Digital (AC-3), DTS, and uncompressed LPCM—can be multiplexed directly into the file alongside the video without conversion.
  • Subtitles: Original DVD bitmap subtitles (VobSub) are preserved in their native format inside the MKV, maintaining original styling, positioning, and language tags.
  • Chapters and Metadata: DVD chapter markers defined within the disc's IFO files can be imported directly into the MKV chapter table, enabling seamless navigation.

Standard Extraction Tools

Tools such as MakeMKV and MKVToolNix are standard utilities used to perform this encapsulation. These tools parse the DVD's title sets, decrypt the content if necessary, and multiplex the native MPEG-2 video and selected audio/subtitle tracks into the .mkv format rapidly, limited only by drive read and write speeds.
