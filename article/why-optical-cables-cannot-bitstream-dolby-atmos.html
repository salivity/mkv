Why Optical Cables Cannot Bitstream Dolby Atmos

Many media players fail to bitstream Dolby Atmos audio from MKV files over an optical cable because the optical (S/PDIF) interface physically lacks the bandwidth and protocol support required for Atmos audio streams. While an MKV file can contain advanced immersive audio formats like Dolby Digital Plus with Atmos or lossless Dolby TrueHD with Atmos, the optical audio standard is fundamentally limited to legacy formats. Consequently, media players either downmix the audio, extract standard core 5.1 audio, or fail to transmit the stream entirely.

Bandwidth Limitations of Optical Audio (S/PDIF)

The optical audio standard (Toslink/SPDIF) was developed in the 1980s and finalized long before object-based audio formats existed. Its maximum bandwidth is strictly limited. It can only transmit:

  • Two channels of uncompressed stereo PCM audio.
  • Compressed 5.1 audio using legacy, lossy codecs: standard Dolby Digital (AC-3) or standard DTS.

Dolby Atmos is not a standalone audio codec; instead, it is an object-based spatial metadata layer that rides on top of either a Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC-3) stream or a Dolby TrueHD stream. Both of these carrier codecs require substantially higher data rates than an optical connection can deliver.

Atmos Delivery Methods in MKV Files

MKV files with Dolby Atmos typically encapsulate audio in one of two formats:

  1. Dolby TrueHD with Dolby Atmos: A lossless, high-bandwidth format commonly extracted from Blu-ray or 4K Ultra HD discs. Optical cables have nowhere near the bandwidth capacity to carry TrueHD.
  2. Dolby Digital Plus with Dolby Atmos (E-AC-3): A lossy, higher-efficiency format commonly used by streaming services. Although compressed, E-AC-3 operates at bitrates and channel layouts that are not supported by the S/PDIF standard.

Why Media Players Fail During Playback

When a media player is set to bitstream audio over an optical cable, the handshake cannot negotiate an Atmos transmission. Depending on the media player's internal logic and software capabilities:

  • Fallback to Standard Dolby Digital (AC-3): Some media players will strip the Atmos metadata and down-convert or fallback to the embedded legacy AC-3 core (5.1), losing all height and object data.
  • Stereo PCM Downmixing: If the player cannot transcode the track, it downmixes the multichannel stream into a basic 2.0 PCM format.
  • Audio Dropouts or Total Silence: If the media player attempts to force the raw, incompatible bitstream over optical without converting it, the receiving AV receiver or soundbar will fail to decode the data, resulting in stuttering, loud noise, or no sound at all.

The Required Solution

To successfully bitstream Dolby Atmos from an MKV file to a receiver or soundbar, an HDMI connection must be used instead of an optical cable:

  • HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel): Supports both Dolby TrueHD (lossless) and Dolby Digital Plus with Atmos.
  • HDMI ARC (Standard Audio Return Channel): Supports compressed Dolby Digital Plus with Atmos, but not lossless Dolby TrueHD.
  • Direct HDMI Connection: Connecting the media player directly to an AV receiver or soundbar's HDMI input provides full support for both TrueHD Atmos and Dolby Digital Plus Atmos.
