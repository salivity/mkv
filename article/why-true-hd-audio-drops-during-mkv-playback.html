Why TrueHD Audio Drops During MKV Playback

Audio dropouts during the playback of MKV files containing high-bitrate Dolby TrueHD tracks typically occur due to hardware processing limitations, HDMI bandwidth constraints, and timestamp synchronization errors within the container. When media players fail to continuously buffer, decode, or pass through the massive volume of data associated with lossless audio, the stream stutters or cuts out entirely.

Bandwidth and Buffer Underruns

Dolby TrueHD is a lossless, variable bitrate codec that can peak at extremely high data rates, especially when combined with Dolby Atmos spatial metadata. Underpowered media players, smart TV system-on-chips (SoCs), or slow local network connections (such as weak Wi-Fi or slow SMB shares) struggle to sustain this continuous throughput. When the device's internal audio buffer empties faster than it can be filled, an audio dropout occurs while the decoder waits for the next valid data packet.

HDMI ARC vs. eARC Limitations

A common cause of audio loss is routing audio through incompatible HDMI connections:

  • Standard HDMI ARC: Lacks the required bandwidth for lossless Dolby TrueHD and is strictly limited to compressed formats like standard Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus, or stereo PCM. Attempting to send TrueHD over ARC causes playback failure, silence, or intermittent signal drops.
  • HDMI eARC / Direct AVR Connection: TrueHD bitstreaming requires either an HDMI 2.1 eARC connection or connecting the media player directly to an AV receiver or compatible soundbar via HDMI before routing the video signal to the display.

Timestamp Gaps from Seamless Branching

Many MKV files are ripped from Blu-ray discs that utilize "seamless branching"—a technique where multiple video and audio segments are joined dynamically on the disc. If the muxing software does not properly calculate and align the Presentation Time Stamps (PTS) during the creation of the MKV file, tiny timing gaps or overlapping packets are introduced into the TrueHD track. While standard decoders can sometimes ignore these micro-gaps, sensitive hardware players lose lock on the audio bitstream and drop sound for several seconds while attempting to resynchronize.

Lack of Native Hardware Passthrough Support

Many lightweight media players, budget streaming sticks, and native smart TV media apps lack dedicated hardware licenses or processing architecture to decode or pass through raw TrueHD bitstreams. When forced to software-decode or transcode high-bitrate MLP (Meridian Lossless Packing) streams on the fly, the processor frequently maxes out, leading to dropped frames and intermittent audio cutouts.
