Can MKV Contain Multichannel DSD Audio?

This article explores whether the Matroska (MKV) multimedia container can encapsulate high-resolution multichannel Direct Stream Digital (DSD) audio streams. While MKV is renowned for its flexibility, encapsulating native 1-bit DSD streams presents specific technical constraints regarding container specifications, encapsulation methods like DSD over PCM (DoP), and playback software compatibility.

Native DSD vs. Matroska Specifications

The Matroska specification does not officially define a standard codec ID for raw 1-bit delta-sigma DSD audio (such as standard DSF or DFF payloads). Unlike standard Pulse Code Modulation (PCM), FLAC, TrueHD, or DTS-HD Master Audio, native DSD streams are fundamentally different in architecture:

  • Native Bitstream Structure: DSD uses a 1-bit sample depth at extremely high sample rates (e.g., 2.8224 MHz for DSD64 or 5.6448 MHz for DSD128).
  • Codec ID Absence: Because standard Matroska specifications lack an explicit A_DSD codec identifier, standard muxers (like MKVToolNix) do not natively accept raw .dsf or .dff files directly without conversion or repackaging.

Methods for Encapsulating DSD in MKV

Despite the lack of a native raw codec ID, high-resolution multichannel DSD can be encapsulated within MKV using specific techniques:

1. DSD over PCM (DoP)

The most standardized method to store DSD inside an MKV container without converting it to standard PCM is via DoP (DSD over PCM):

  • DSD 1-bit data is sliced into 16-bit chunks and paired with an 8-bit marker inside a standard 24-bit PCM stream.
  • The MKV container recognizes the track as standard high-resolution PCM (e.g., 24-bit at 176.4 kHz for DSD64).
  • A DoP-compatible DAC detects the 8-bit marker and decodes the stream as pure bit-perfect DSD rather than PCM.

2. Multichannel DoP Support

Matroska fully supports multichannel audio mapping (e.g., 5.0, 5.1, or 7.1 surround sound configurations). When using DoP:

  • Multichannel DSD tracks can be multiplexed as a multichannel 24-bit PCM track.
  • Channel assignments (Front Left, Center, Front Right, Surround, LFE) remain intact according to standard Matroska audio channel guidelines.

3. Lossless Conversion to Multichannel PCM/FLAC

If DoP is not used, the common approach for placing multichannel DSD into MKV is converting the 1-bit DSD stream into a high-resolution, uncompressed Multichannel PCM or lossless Multichannel FLAC stream (typically 24-bit/88.2 kHz or 24-bit/176.4 kHz). While this alters the underlying 1-bit delta-sigma format, it preserves the high-frequency content and dynamic range with universal hardware and software compatibility.

Playback and Hardware Compatibility

Encapsulating DSD in MKV impacts downstream playback:

  • Software Players: Players like MPV or Foobar2000 (with appropriate plugins) can decode or pass through DoP streams housed in MKV. Media players lacking DoP recognition will interpret the audio as static or white noise because they treat the payload as standard PCM.
  • AV Receivers and DACs: For true DSD playback, the connected digital-to-analog converter (DAC) must explicitly support multichannel DoP over a compatible interface, such as HDMI or dedicated multichannel USB connections.

Summary

An MKV container cannot natively encapsulate raw DSD streams in an officially standardized A_DSD format. However, it can encapsulate high-resolution multichannel DSD via DSD over PCM (DoP) formatted within standard PCM tracks, retaining bit-perfect multichannel DSD playback when paired with DoP-compliant software and hardware.
