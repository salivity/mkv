Can MKV Hold 7.1 LPCM Audio Without Downmixing?

The Matroska (MKV) multimedia container fully supports multichannel 7.1 Linear Pulse Code Modulation (LPCM) audio streams natively, without requiring downmixing to stereo or 5.1 channels. Because MKV functions as an open, highly flexible container format, it can encapsulate raw, uncompressed 8-channel audio tracks while preserving the original channel layout, bit depth, and sample rate of the source material.

Native Support for Multichannel PCM in MKV

The Matroska specification includes native definitions for raw, uncompressed audio formats. In an MKV file, uncompressed PCM audio is typically defined by codecs such as A_PCM/INT/LIT (for integer little-endian format) or A_PCM/FLOAT/IEEE (for floating-point audio).

When muxing a 7.1 LPCM stream into an MKV container, the muxer (such as MKVToolNix) writes discrete channels into the stream headers. The eight audio channels—typically configured as Front Left, Front Right, Center, Low-Frequency Effects (LFE), Surround Left, Surround Right, Back Left, and Back Right—remain completely independent. No processing, re-encoding, or downmixing occurs during the packaging process.

Playback and Hardware Compatibility

While an MKV container can store 7.1 LPCM without modification, playing the 8-channel stream correctly depends on your software configuration and hardware chain:

  • Software Players: Modern media players such as VLC, mpv, and MPC-HC/MPC-BE natively parse 7.1 LPCM streams inside MKV files and can route all eight discrete channels to your sound configuration.
  • Audio Passthrough and HDMI: LPCM 7.1 requires high-bandwidth connections, such as HDMI (eARC or standard HDMI to an AV receiver). Optical (TOSLINK) and SPDIF coaxial cables lack the bandwidth for multichannel uncompressed PCM and will force either stereo downmixing or lossy re-encoding.
  • AV Receivers (AVR): An AV receiver connected via HDMI will receive the uncompressed multichannel PCM directly and display it as "Multi-Channel PCM" or "PCM 7.1," outputting each channel to the corresponding speaker.

LPCM vs. Lossless Alternatives in MKV

While 7.1 LPCM works natively in MKV, it produces significantly larger file sizes because it is completely uncompressed. For this reason, users often convert 7.1 LPCM to lossless formats like FLAC, Dolby TrueHD, or DTS-HD Master Audio before muxing into MKV. These lossless formats preserve the exact bit-for-bit audio quality and discrete 7.1 channel separation of the original LPCM stream while reducing overall file size by 30% to 60%.
