Can MKV Encapsulate Cinepak and Indeo Video?

The Matroska (MKV) container can fully encapsulate legacy video formats such as Cinepak and Intel Indeo. Thanks to its flexible, codec-agnostic architecture, MKV is not restricted to modern compression standards like H.264, HEVC, or AV1. By utilizing backward-compatibility mapping, MKV allows users to remux vintage video files from legacy containers like AVI into the MKV format without needing to re-encode the underlying streams.

How Matroska Supports Legacy Codecs

The Matroska specification handles legacy Video for Windows (VFW) codecs through a dedicated Codec ID: V_MS/VFW/FOURCC.

When encapsulating older formats, the MKV container stores the standard Windows BITMAPINFOHEADER structure within the track's private data (CodecPrivate). This header contains the original FourCC (Four-Character Code) used to identify the codec.

Cinepak in MKV

Cinepak, developed in the early 1990s and identified by the FourCC cvid, stores cleanly inside an MKV container. When muxed, the stream retains its original compressed frames, and the MKV track flags the video stream with V_MS/VFW/FOURCC along with the cvid identifier.

Intel Indeo in MKV

Intel Indeo video formats—including Indeo Video 3 (IV31, IV32), Indeo Video 4 (IV41), and Indeo Video 5 (IV50)—are supported in the exact same manner. The MKV container preserves the raw bitstream and relies on the FourCC identifier inside the container metadata to signal the decoder.

How to Remux Cinepak and Indeo to MKV

You can remux legacy AVI or QuickTime files containing Cinepak or Indeo into MKV using standard tools like FFmpeg or MKVToolNix without re-encoding:

ffmpeg -i legacy_input.avi -c copy output.mkv

Using the -c copy (stream copy) flag ensures that the video stream is directly transferred into the MKV container without any loss of quality or change in compression.

Playback Considerations

While MKV can store Cinepak and Indeo streams without issue, playback depends on the media player's decoding capabilities:

  • Modern Open-Source Players: Players such as VLC, MPV, and MPC-HC utilize the libavcodec library (from the FFmpeg project), which includes native software decoders for Cinepak and Indeo (Indeo 2, 3, 4, and 5). These players can play Cinepak and Indeo MKV files out of the box on modern operating systems.
  • System-Default Players: Native OS players (such as the default Windows Media Player or macOS QuickTime) may fail to play these files unless the corresponding system-level DirectShow filters or decoders are installed.
