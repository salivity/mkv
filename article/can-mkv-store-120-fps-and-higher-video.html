Can MKV Store 120 FPS and Higher Video?

The Matroska (MKV) container format fully supports high-frame-rate (HFR) video at 120 frames per second (fps) and even higher. Because Matroska is an open-standard, extensible container designed with flexible timecode architecture rather than fixed frame rate limits, it can encapsulate virtually any frame rate. This guide explains how MKV handles ultra-high frame rates, the codec standards involved, and the practical requirements for smooth playback.

MKV Timecode Architecture

Traditional video containers sometimes rely on fixed time bases that limit framerate options. Matroska, however, uses an arbitrary timestamp system for each data block.

  • Timestamp Precision: By default, Matroska tracks frames with millisecond precision (1 kHz timebase), which easily supports 120 fps (roughly 8.33 milliseconds per frame), 144 fps, and 240 fps.
  • Nanosecond Scaling: MKV includes a TimestampScale header element that can increase precision down to the nanosecond level. This ensures exact synchronization even at extreme frame rates of 1,000 fps or higher, as well as for variable frame rate (VFR) content.

Video Codec Requirements

While the MKV container itself can hold arbitrary frame rates, the video stream inside must be encoded with a codec that supports high frame rates at the target resolution:

  • H.264 / AVC: Supports high frame rates, though high resolutions (such as 4K at 120 fps) may exceed the maximum decode levels of standard H.264 profiles.
  • H.265 / HEVC: Native support for 120 fps up to 8K resolutions when using high tiers and levels (such as Level 5.2 and Level 6.2).
  • AV1 and VP9: Both modern open codecs support 120 fps and 240 fps profiles, making them ideal for high-motion gaming captures and HFR mastering inside MKV.

Playback and Hardware Considerations

Storing a 120+ fps video in an MKV file is only half the equation; rendering it accurately requires compatible hardware and software:

  1. Hardware Decoding: The graphics card (GPU) or processor (CPU) must possess hardware decoders capable of processing 120 or more frames per second at the file's native resolution.
  2. Display Capabilities: To perceive the 120+ fps output, the display must support a refresh rate of 120 Hz, 144 Hz, 240 Hz, or higher, connected via high-bandwidth interfaces like HDMI 2.1 or DisplayPort 1.4/2.1.
  3. Media Player Software: Modern media players like MPV, VLC, and MPC-HC (with LAV Filters) read MKV timestamp data accurately and can present frames at the display's native refresh rate without dropping frames.

MKV presents no structural bottleneck for high-frame-rate video. Any operational limitations will stem from the chosen video codec's profile limits or the hardware used to decode and display the content.
