MKV Audio Desync When Cutting Without Keyframes

Cutting an MKV file without aligning the cut point to a keyframe is one of the most common reasons for audio and video desynchronization. This issue arises because modern compressed video streams rely on inter-frame dependencies, meaning non-keyframe video frames cannot be decoded independently. When a cut is made between keyframes without re-encoding, video decoders drop or freeze invalid frames while the audio continues to play, creating an offset between the audio and video timestamps within the Matroska (MKV) container.

Understanding Keyframes and Inter-Frame Compression

Digital video codecs such as H.264, H.265 (HEVC), and AV1 use temporal compression to save bandwidth. Instead of storing every frame as a complete picture, the video is organized into a Group of Pictures (GOP) comprising three main frame types:

  • I-Frames (Keyframes/Intra-frames): Complete, standalone images that do not require data from other frames to decode.
  • P-Frames (Predicted frames): Frames that only store changes relative to previous frames.
  • B-Frames (Bi-directional predicted frames): Frames that rely on both preceding and subsequent frames to reconstruct the image.

When you cut a video using "stream copy" mode (trimming without re-encoding), the software extracts raw packets directly from the container.

Why Audio Desync Occurs at Non-Keyframe Cuts

1. Incomplete Video Decoding Chains

If a cut starts on a P-frame or a B-frame, the decoder lacks the preceding I-frame required to construct the initial images. Consequently, the media player cannot display these initial frames. Most media players handle these missing reference frames by discarding all video packets until the next valid keyframe arrives.

2. Continuous Audio Stream vs. Dropped Video Packets

Audio streams (such as AAC, AC3, or DTS) are divided into very small, independent packets that do not rely on complex temporal GOP structures. When a cut is made, the audio begins decoding and playing immediately from the designated start time. Because the player must discard the corrupted video frames leading up to the next keyframe while playing the audio continuously, the video appears late, leading to an immediate audio lead (audio ahead of video).

3. Presentation Timestamp (PTS) and Start-Time Mismatches

The MKV container synchronizes streams using Presentation Timestamps (PTS), which tell the player exactly when to render each frame and audio sample. When cutting without keyframe alignment:

  • The video stream's valid start timestamp shifts forward to the first decodable keyframe.
  • The audio stream's start timestamp remains at the exact split point.
  • Depending on how the splitting tool handles timestamps, it may leave a timestamp gap or reset the audio PTS to zero without shifting the video, resulting in persistent synchronization offsets throughout the remainder of the clip.

How to Prevent Desynchronization

To avoid audio desync when editing MKV files:

  • Cut Strictly on Keyframes: Ensure the editing software snaps cut markers directly to I-frames. This preserves clean playback without requiring re-encoding.
  • Smart Rendering: Use tools capable of "smart cutting," which re-encodes only the small GOP surrounding the cut point to create a new keyframe while leaving the rest of the stream untouched.
  • Full Re-encoding: If precise, frame-accurate cuts are required on non-keyframes, fully re-encode the video stream so every cut point begins with a newly generated keyframe.
