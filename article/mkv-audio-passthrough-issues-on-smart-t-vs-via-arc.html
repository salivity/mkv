MKV Audio Passthrough Issues on Smart TVs via ARC

Many smart TV owners encounter issues when attempting to pass multichannel audio from MKV files to an AV receiver or soundbar via HDMI ARC (Audio Return Channel), often resulting in stereo downmixing or no sound at all. This issue typically stems from a combination of HDMI ARC bandwidth limitations, missing audio codec licenses on modern smart TVs, and internal media player restrictions that prevent the TV from sending raw audio bitstreams to external sound systems.

1. Lack of Codec Licensing (DTS and Dolby)

The most common reason for audio failure with MKV files is the specific audio codec inside the container. Many MKV video files use DTS, DTS-HD Master Audio, or Dolby TrueHD tracks. Several major TV manufacturers (such as Samsung and modern LG models) have dropped support and licensing for DTS codecs entirely. If a TV lacks a DTS license, its internal operating system will either refuse to decode the track, output silence, or downmix it to basic 2-channel PCM stereo before outputting it over HDMI ARC.

2. HDMI ARC Bandwidth Limitations

Standard HDMI ARC has a limited bandwidth of roughly 1 to 1.5 Mbps. While this is sufficient for uncompressed 2.0 stereo PCM and legacy compressed 5.1 bitstream formats (like standard Dolby Digital AC3 and basic DTS), it cannot transport:

  • Uncompressed multichannel PCM (5.1 or 7.1)
  • Lossless multichannel formats like Dolby TrueHD or DTS-HD MA
  • High-bitrate multichannel audio found in high-definition MKV rips

If an MKV file contains a lossless format and the TV attempts to decode it internally to multichannel PCM, ARC will not have the bandwidth to transmit all channels, forcing a downmix to 2.0 stereo. Passing lossless multichannel formats requires HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel) on both the TV and the audio receiver.

3. Built-in Media Player Constraints

The native media player apps integrated into smart TV operating systems (Tizen, webOS, Android TV/Google TV) are often not configured for true raw bitstream passthrough. When playing files directly from a connected USB drive or local network share (DLNA/SMB), the TV's internal audio pipeline often intercepts the audio stream, decodes it locally, and sends a modified stereo signal over ARC rather than passing the untouched digital stream directly to the receiver.

4. Misconfigured Audio Output Settings

Smart TVs usually default to safe audio settings to prevent compatibility errors with basic soundbars. If the TV's digital sound output is set to "PCM" instead of "Bitstream", "Auto", or "Pass-Through", the TV will internally decode all audio and send a 2-channel PCM signal over standard ARC.

How to Resolve the Issue

  • Switch Audio Settings: Set the TV's digital audio output format to Passthrough or Bitstream instead of PCM.
  • Transcode Audio Tracks: Use media tools to convert the MKV audio stream to standard 5.1 Dolby Digital (AC3) or Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC3), which are universally supported across ARC connections.
  • Use a Dedicated External Media Player: Connect a dedicated streaming box (such as an Apple TV 4K, Nvidia Shield, or dedicated media player) directly to the AV receiver or soundbar's HDMI input rather than plugging directly into the TV. This bypasses the TV's internal audio limitations entirely.
