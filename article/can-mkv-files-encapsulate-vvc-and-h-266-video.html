Can MKV Files Encapsulate VVC and H.266 Video?

The Matroska (MKV) multimedia container format fully supports the encapsulation of Versatile Video Coding (VVC), officially standardized as ITU-T H.266 and ISO/IEC 23090-3. Thanks to updates in the Matroska specifications and leading open-source multiplexing software, users can package VVC elementary streams alongside modern audio and subtitle formats inside an MKV container. This article explains how MKV handles H.266/VVC video, the technical requirements for muxing, and current playback compatibility.

How MKV Handles VVC/H.266 Codecs

Matroska is a flexible, format-agnostic container designed to hold virtually any audio, video, or subtitle format. To support VVC, the Matroska specifications define standard codec identifiers—primarily V_MPEGH/ISO/VVC—which allow the container to identify the stream and properly read its parameter sets, frame rates, and bitstream structure.

MKV encapsulates VVC in a packetized NAL unit format (similar to how it stores H.264/AVC and H.265/HEVC). This ensures that keyframes, timestamps, aspect ratios, and color metadata (such as HDR10 or Dolby Vision metadata carried within VVC) are mapped properly within the Matroska header block structure.

Muxing VVC into MKV

Leading open-source video processing and container management tools support remuxing VVC elementary bitstreams (typically files with .vvc or .266 extensions) into .mkv files:

  • MKVToolNix: Modern releases of MKVToolNix support reading raw VVC/H.266 video streams and packaging them into MKV files with custom tracks, tags, and chapters.
  • FFmpeg: Recent builds of FFmpeg compiled with VVC bitstream parsing capabilities can remux or encode VVC streams directly into an MKV output file using standard command-line operations.
  • GPAC / MP4Box to MKV: Users who have VVC content in MP4 containers can extract or directly remux those streams into MKV containers without re-encoding the underlying video.

Benefits of Using MKV for VVC Content

Using MKV for H.266/VVC video offers several practical advantages:

  • Broad Audio Support: MKV can pair highly efficient VVC video with high-end or lossless audio formats such as Dolby TrueHD, DTS-HD Master Audio, FLAC, and Opus.
  • Advanced Subtitles: Unlike some competing containers, MKV allows styling-heavy subtitle formats like SSA/ASS, PGS, and SRT to be embedded seamlessly with VVC video.
  • Future-Proofing: As VVC delivers up to 50% better compression efficiency compared to HEVC at equivalent visual quality, packaging it in an extensible container like MKV ensures long-term media preservation.

Current Playback Support

While the MKV container supports VVC, decoding the video stream inside requires media players equipped with compatible software decoders (such as vvdec or the native FFmpeg VVC decoder). Players such as mpv and modern developer builds of VLC are capable of demuxing and playing VVC-in-MKV files, though high-resolution playback (such as 4K or 8K) currently depends on CPU power until dedicated hardware decoding becomes standard in consumer GPUs and processors.
