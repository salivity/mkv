Can MKV Containers Store EIA-608 Closed Captions?

The Matroska (MKV) container can store EIA-608 closed captioning streams, but primarily as embedded data within the video stream rather than as a standalone, dedicated subtitle track. While MKV natively supports various text and bitmap subtitle formats like SRT, ASS, and PGS, standard EIA-608 (Line 21) captions are preserved within the container by remaining encoded inside the video stream’s user data or Supplemental Enhancement Information (SEI) NAL units.

Embedded Storage in Video Streams

EIA-608 captions were originally designed for analog broadcast television and embedded into Line 21 of the vertical blanking interval. In modern digital video, EIA-608 byte pairs are carried directly inside the video stream:

  • H.264 / AVC and H.265 / HEVC: Captions reside within SEI user data packets.
  • MPEG-2: Captions are stored within the MPEG-2 picture user data.

When you remux or package these video streams into an MKV container using tools like MKVToolNix or FFmpeg, the container encapsulates the entire video bitstream. Consequently, the EIA-608 caption data is preserved completely intact inside the video track.

Standalone Subtitle Track Limitations

The Matroska specification does not feature a widely adopted, native Codec ID dedicated solely to raw, standalone EIA-608/708 binary streams (such as raw .scc or .mcc data tracks). Unlike MP4 (which can use c608 or c708 sample entries), MKV relies on standard text or timed-text formats for separate subtitle tracks.

If you attempt to demux EIA-608 captions out of the video stream and add them back into an MKV file as an independent subtitle track, they must typically be converted into a native Matroska subtitle format, such as:

  • UTF-8 Plain Text (S_TEXT/UTF8 / SRT)
  • SubStation Alpha (S_TEXT/ASS)
  • WebVTT (S_TEXT/WEBVTT)

Playback and Compatibility

Media players handle EIA-608 data stored in MKV containers in two different ways:

  1. Hardware/Software Decoders with CC Support: Advanced media players (such as VLC, MPV, or specific broadcast-grade decoders) can inspect the video bitstream's SEI/user data within the MKV, extract the EIA-608 closed captions in real time, and render them over the video.
  2. Standard Player Fallback: Basic players that only scan for dedicated MKV subtitle tracks will not automatically detect embedded EIA-608 captions unless their internal video decoder explicitly parses caption packets.

For maximum compatibility across all consumer devices and software players, the standard practice when working with MKV files is using tools like CCExtractor or FFmpeg to extract embedded EIA-608 streams and convert them into standard SRT or ASS subtitle tracks within the container.
