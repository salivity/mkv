Can MKV Store HD Video and Uncompressed Audio?

The Matroska Video (MKV) container format fully supports storing high-definition and ultra-high-definition video alongside uncompressed, multi-channel studio-grade audio. Unlike rigid proprietary formats, MKV acts as an open-standard universal envelope capable of encapsulating virtually any combination of video, audio, and subtitle streams without requiring re-encoding or compression. This article explains how MKV accommodates raw multi-channel audio tracks alongside high-definition video streams and why it is a standard choice for high-fidelity media preservation.

How the Matroska Container Works

An MKV file is not a video or audio codec; it is an extensible multimedia container based on EBML (Extensible Binary Meta Language). Because EBML functions similarly to binary XML, MKV has no inherent architectural limitations regarding the type of codec, sample rate, bit depth, or channel count it can hold. It merely wraps the raw bitstreams into a single file with precise synchronization timestamps.

High-Definition Video Support

MKV natively supports all modern and legacy high-definition video standards, including:

  • Resolutions: Full HD (1080p), 4K UHD (2160p), 8K, and custom non-standard resolutions.
  • Codecs: H.264 (AVC), H.265 (HEVC), AV1, VP9, and intra-frame production formats such as Apple ProRes and Avid DNxHD/DNxHR.
  • Color Spaces: Standard Dynamic Range (SDR) as well as High Dynamic Range (HDR10, HDR10+, and Dolby Vision) with 10-bit or 12-bit color depths.

Uncompressed Multi-Channel Audio Capabilities

MKV easily handles uncompressed studio audio formats, providing bit-for-bit fidelity with the original master recordings:

  • Linear PCM (LPCM): MKV can store raw, uncompressed Pulse Code Modulation audio. This includes standard stereo as well as multi-channel layouts (e.g., 5.1, 7.1, or custom surround configurations).
  • High Sample Rates and Bit Depths: Studio-grade audio standards—such as 24-bit/96 kHz, 24-bit/192 kHz, or 32-bit floating-point audio—are fully supported without downsampling.
  • Lossless Alternatives: Alongside pure uncompressed PCM, MKV supports bit-perfect lossless compression codecs such as FLAC, Dolby TrueHD (including Dolby Atmos metadata), and DTS-HD Master Audio.

Key Advantages for Archival and Studio Workflows

  1. No Quality Degradation: Since the container multiplexes the elementary streams without modifying the underlying data, the video and audio remain identical to the source master.
  2. Multiple Audio Tracks: An MKV file can hold dozens of separate audio tracks simultaneously, allowing a single file to contain the original multi-channel uncompressed mix, alternate language tracks, commentary, and stereo downmixes.
  3. Broad Software Compatibility: Modern media players (such as VLC, MPV, and Kodi) and professional demuxing tools (such as FFmpeg and MKVToolNix) natively support playback and extraction of uncompressed audio streams from MKV files.
